A wedding gift is arranged paradoxically: you choose it for two people who have just merged all their belongings and therefore already own most of the things one gives.

Tableware, appliances, textiles — a couple either has these or has bought them deliberately for the new home. The gift duplicates what they chose themselves.

Painting is one of the few exceptions. It is not bought "for the household", so it does not compete with what the couple has already acquired.

## Why a painting

**You never have too much of it.** Unlike a fourth set of glasses, a painting does not end up in a cupboard. Empty walls in a new shared space are something newlyweds genuinely lack.

**It outlives the decor.** Furniture is changed in ten years, the place is redecorated twice. An oil painting stays. In twenty years it will recall not so much the giver as the period when everything began.

**It is a shared first thing.** Much in a couple's home came to them separately — his books, her dishes. A painting given at the wedding becomes the first object that belongs to both at once.

**It has a history.** An original painting carries the artist's name, its context, its provenance. Not a nameless object from a shop but a thing with a biography that can be told to guests.

## How to choose a work for a couple

The main difficulty: you are choosing not by your own taste, nor by one person's, but by what suits two.

**Look at a neutral palette.** Warm earth tones and a muted palette fit most interiors and impose no mood. A bright, dramatic work may please one of the couple and irritate the other.

**Avoid the too personal.** A portrait of a stranger, a recognisable view of a particular city, a religious subject — anything that narrows things. If you guess wrong, the work will not settle in.

**Figurative is safer than abstract.** Abstract painting either appeals strongly or not at all. A figurative work with recognisable forms is accepted more widely.

**No unsettling subjects.** What works in a gallery is not necessarily what you want to see daily above the dining table in the first year of marriage.

**Size with margin.** The main mistake is taking a work that is too small. Above a sofa or in a dining room you need a format from 80 cm, or the painting is lost.

## The joint gift

The wisest option for a wedding, and here is why.

An original painting of medium format costs eight hundred to two thousand euros. For a single guest that is a lot; for a group of five or six it is an ordinary contribution, like an appliance or a piece of furniture.

And the gift comes out incomparably better: instead of five separate things that get lost among others — one object that stays for good and recalls everyone who chipped in.

It is simple to organise: one person chooses the work and agrees it with the rest, the money is collected, the painting ordered for the couple. The certificate can be issued with the names of the givers.

## What to avoid

**Do not give a commissioned portrait of the couple.** It seems touching but is risky: taste in portraits is personal, and if the result disappoints, the gift becomes a burden. Besides, painting from a photograph is rarely good painting.

**Do not take a "wedding" subject.** Doves, rings, hearts are decoration, not art, and live exactly until the first redecoration.

**Do not go by your own interior.** You are choosing for their home, not yours. The colour of your walls has nothing to do with it.

**Do not impose a choice on the couple.** If you are unsure of the newlyweds' taste, the best gift is a voucher letting them choose for themselves. That is no less generous than a finished work.

## Practical matters

**Timing.** A finished work can be collected or shipped in a few days. If the gift is for the wedding date, allow a week for delivery within the EU.

**Packing.** For a gift you can ask for neutral packaging without markings — so the surprise holds until it is handed over.

**Framing.** A work on a stretcher is ready to hang straight away. A frame is a separate decision, better left to the couple: they will choose one to suit their space.

**A certificate with names.** The document of authenticity can be issued noting to whom and on what occasion the work was given. That makes the painting a kind of family piece from the outset.
