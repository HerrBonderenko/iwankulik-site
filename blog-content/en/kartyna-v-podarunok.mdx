A gift that outlives the person giving it is rare. Most things break, go out of fashion, or simply become dull. An oil painting on canvas does none of these: under normal conditions it hangs for centuries, and each generation that follows sees something of its own in it.

That does not make it a universal gift. But for a handful of particular occasions it suits better than anything else.

## When a painting fits

**A wedding.** The classic occasion. A couple is furnishing their first shared space, and the painting becomes the first object they chose for something other than its function. In twenty years it will recall not the giver but that period of their life.

**An anniversary.** A round one especially. For someone who already has everything, a gift is hard to find. An original solves it: you cannot buy it in the shop where everyone buys.

**A housewarming.** Empty walls in a new home are an occasion that explains itself. Only one thing matters here: knowing the room's palette at least roughly.

**A relationship anniversary.** Not necessarily a round one. A painting bought "just because" is often valued more than a gift tied to a date.

**[A corporate occasion](/en/blog/zhyvopys-v-ofisi).** For a director, a partner, on retirement. Here an original works as a mark of respect — unlike a branded gift with a logo on it.

## When to think twice

Honestly about the other side.

**If you do not know their taste.** Painting is a personal thing. Someone who likes minimalism will not welcome dense figuration, and the reverse holds too. If you know nothing about the taste, a voucher and letting them choose is the better move.

**If there is no wall.** It sounds odd but it happens: a small flat where every metre is taken, or someone who has just sold up and is moving. The painting becomes a burden rather than a pleasure.

**If they are mid-renovation.** [Fresh plaster and concrete give off moisture for months](/en/blog/de-vishaty-kartynu), and that harms paintings. Better to wait until the room has dried out.

## What size

The most common mistake with a gift is choosing a work that is too small. It seems that a large painting is "too much", when in fact a small format gets lost on the wall and looks accidental.

The rule of thumb is simple: a painting above a sofa should occupy about two-thirds of its width. A 200 cm sofa — a work of 120 to 140 cm. Above a chest of drawers or a console, roughly the width of the furniture itself.

If you do not know the room's dimensions, anything from 60 × 80 cm is a safe choice. It works both alone on a smaller wall and grouped with others.

## What subject

A few rules that rarely disappoint.

**Avoid the too specific.** A portrait of an unknown person or a recognisable view of a particular city narrows things: if you guess wrong, the painting will not settle in.

**Look at the palette, not the subject.** A work in warm ochre tones [will fit most interiors](/en/blog/kartyna-v-interyeri). A cool blue-green demands the right surroundings.

**Figurative is safer than abstract.** Abstract painting either appeals strongly or not at all — there is rarely a middle. A figurative work with recognisable forms is accepted more widely.

**Avoid unsettling subjects.** What looks good in a gallery is not always what you want to see daily above the dining table.

## What it costs

The price range for contemporary European painting of medium format runs from several hundred to several thousand euros. A work of 100 × 140 cm by an artist with a thirty-year exhibition history is priced differently from a beginner's study, and rightly so.

For a gift this often proves cheaper than expected. When several people [club together](/en/blog/kartyna-na-vesillya) — and for weddings and anniversaries they constantly do — an original painting lands in the same budget as an appliance or a piece of furniture. It simply lasts longer.

## How it looks from the recipient's side

A point often underestimated: a gifted painting brings with it not only itself but a history.

Whoever receives an original knows the painter's name. They can open his website, see other works, understand which cycle their canvas belongs to. They can write to the artist. That is not the same as receiving an object from a shop, which has neither author nor history.

Add to that the certificate of authenticity with its number in the catalogue of works — a document that passes on with the painting and evidences its provenance permanently.

## Practical matters

**Timing.** A finished work can be collected or shipped within a few days. If the gift is for a specific date, allow a week for delivery within the EU and two weeks beyond it.

**Framing.** Clarify this in advance: a work on a stretcher is ready to hang straight away; framed it looks more finished but costs more and weighs more.

**Packing.** For a gift you can ask for neutral packaging without markings — so that what is inside is not obvious.

**If it does not suit.** Terms are agreed individually. A sensible artist wants the work to find its owner rather than hang where it is not loved.
