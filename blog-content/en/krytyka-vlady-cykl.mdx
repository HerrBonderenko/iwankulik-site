Four of my five cycles began with a text. Bulgakov, Cervantes, Orwell, the Polish chronicle with Stańczyk — each had an author who had already formulated everything, and what remained for me was to find a visual form for it.

The fifth cycle arose differently. It has no book, no named hero and no plot to refer the viewer to.

## How it began

I noticed that one construction kept recurring in works from the other cycles, a construction that did not belong to those cycles.

Someone is seated. Someone stands beside them. Between them a table, a threshold, the back of a chair — anything that marks a boundary. The seated one looks at the standing one, or does not look at all, and that decides everything.

This construction appeared in Bulgakov, in Orwell, with Stańczyk — but it was not about Woland, or the pigs, or the king. It stood on its own.

So I began painting it separately.

## What the cycle is about

Mechanics, not politics.

No particular power interests me — no country, party or era. These works are not about who is right at the moment, nor about who ought to be condemned. Tying them to events would make them posters, and a poster lives exactly as long as the event does.

Something else interests me: what happens to a person when they are granted the right to decide for others.

It is not an instant transformation. Nobody becomes someone else at once. What changes is bearing — literally, physically. How a person holds their head, where they look while being addressed, how much room they occupy. That can be painted, and it is what I paint.

## Why the figures are nameless

There are no portraits in the cycle and no recognisable faces.

The reason is simple: the moment a specific person appears, the painting stops being about the mechanism and becomes about them. The viewer starts recalling who it is, agreeing or disagreeing — and the construction itself drops out of view.

So the figures are built so that they cannot be identified with anyone. Not types and not symbols, simply people in particular positions relative to one another.

Sometimes the person the decision concerns is not in the frame at all. They are beyond the edge of the canvas — that too is part of the construction.

## What recurs in the cycle

**The table.** [The same motif as in *Animal Farm*](/en/blog/skotoferma-cykl), but stripped here. No animals and no satire — just an object dividing the space in two.

**A difference in height.** One higher, one lower. The simplest way to show hierarchy, and it works even when both figures are seated.

**A gaze that passes by.** The one who decides almost never looks at the one the decision concerns. He looks at the table, at papers, to the side. This is not indifference but technical necessity: to decide, one has to stop seeing.

**Empty space behind.** Behind the standing figure there is usually nothing. He came alone and will leave alone.

## Why the cycle does not end

The other four cycles have a natural limit: the text is finite, and although it can be reread endlessly, the number of situations in it is bounded.

This cycle has no limit. Every few years an occasion arises — not because I look for one, but because the situation repeats.

That is the most unpleasant thing about it. A work painted twenty years ago is regularly read by viewers as a comment on something that happened last month. I have grown used to it, but each time it is a reminder that the construction is alive.

## The link to the other cycles

This cycle is the common denominator beneath the rest.

[Woland in Bulgakov](/en/blog/majster-i-margaryta-cykl) puts people to the test and watches how they behave when the stakes are real. Orwell's pigs travel from equality to the table with the men. Stańczyk knows the truth and is permitted to speak it precisely because it changes nothing. Don Quixote acts in defiance of everything and is gently neutralised — not by force but by not being taken seriously.

The same construction everywhere, only under different names. *Critique of Power* is that construction without the names.

## On materials

The works of the cycle come in various formats — there is no firm requirement here, unlike Don Quixote with his vertical or *Animal Farm* with the horizontal of the table.

The palette is mostly restrained. Brightness is not needed here: the construction should read on its own, not through a colour effect.

The technique is the same as in the others: oil on linen, multi-layered painting with glazes, months of work on a large canvas.
