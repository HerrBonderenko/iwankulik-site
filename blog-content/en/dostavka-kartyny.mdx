The most common reason people do not buy a painting abroad is neither the price nor doubt about authorship. It is the fear that the work will arrive damaged or get stuck at customs.

Both risks are real and both are manageable. Here is how shipping a painting works.

## How a canvas is packed

An oil painting is sensitive to two things: pressure on the surface and abrupt temperature changes. Correct packing addresses both.

**First layer — neutral material.** Silicone-coated paper or smooth film that will not stick to the paint. Ordinary bubble wrap must not go directly against the painting: after a few days in warmth it leaves the imprint of the bubbles on the surface.

**Second layer — rigid board.** Front and back. It holds the geometry and absorbs incidental pressure. This is the most important layer: without it, any knock to the box transfers straight to the canvas.

**Third layer — cushioning.** Now bubble wrap, several turns. Its job is to damp vibration.

**Outside — a rigid box.** For formats up to 80 cm a reinforced double-wall corrugated box is enough. For large works from a metre up, a wooden crate. This is not over-caution: a large box without a frame collapses under its own weight when stacked.

**The corners.** The most vulnerable point. Foam corner protectors are compulsory — the corners take the impact in a fall.

A framed work is packed differently: the frame projects and takes the impact first. Glass, if present, is either removed or taped crosswise.

A wooden crate for a large format adds €50–150 depending on size.

Insurance usually runs at 0.5–1% of the declared value.

## Insuring the shipment

The parcel is insured for the full value of the work. This is standard, and a seller's refusal to insure should give you pause.

Worth knowing: most carriers have a liability limit without separate insurance — typically a few hundred euros. For a painting worth two thousand that is not enough.

Taking out insurance requires proof of value: the artist's invoice and [the certificate of authenticity](/en/blog/sertyfikat-avtentychnosti). So these documents must be in hand before dispatch, not after.

[Photographing before packing](/en/blog/yak-fotohrafuvaty-kartynu) is a compulsory part of the process. Shots of the work from all sides, including the reverse, plus images of the packing itself. Without them a claim is hard to substantiate.

## Customs

Within the European Union there are no customs formalities — the painting travels as an ordinary insured parcel.

Outside the EU a declaration is required. And here is a detail that is rarely known: **original works of art pass at a reduced rate almost everywhere**.

The logic is simple: states distinguish an original painting from a manufactured good. In the United Kingdom original artworks carry a reduced rate of import VAT. In the United States original paintings by a living artist are generally admitted duty-free. Switzerland applies a reduced rate.

Rules change, so the current position is worth checking before purchase — but this is the order of things.

**What the declaration needs:**
- An invoice stating the value
- A description of the work: medium, dimensions, year, artist
- The certificate of authenticity
- The tariff code for original paintings

The artist provides all the documents. Completing the declaration is usually handled by the carrier.

## Who pays

The standard arrangement: the buyer covers shipping and insurance, the seller prepares the documents.

Duty and import VAT, where they arise, are paid by the recipient on delivery. The carrier usually invoices this separately.

Worth settling before purchase so there are no surprises on arrival.

## What to do if it arrives damaged

The sequence that saves the situation.

**Do not accept the parcel without inspecting it.** You are entitled to open it in front of the courier.

**If the box is damaged, record it on the waybill** before you sign. Without that note a claim is nearly hopeless.

**Photograph everything:** the exterior of the box, each layer of packing as you open it, and the work itself.

**Contact the seller immediately**, without delay. You file the claim together.

In practice, damage with proper packing is rare. But the sequence is better known in advance.
